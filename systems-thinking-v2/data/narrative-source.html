Where five of six agreed

The engine

Joann, Sly, Henry, Lia and Ola each drew this loop without seeing the others. Sly put it best on the call: node for node — different words, same loop and same outcome. Nobody drew a minus anywhere in it, which is why it compounds.

The five versions differ only in resolution. Sly and Henry drew all seven steps including trust. Joann folded trust into sustained engagement. Lia drew four nodes, Ola three. None of them contradict each other — the shorter ones are the same path with intermediate steps collapsed.

Where we actually diverged

The brakes

This is the real finding. We did not disagree about the engine — we each found a different thing slowing it down. These are not competing theories. They are four separate constraints acting on one flywheel, and a complete map needs all four.

B · ECONOMICS3 of 6

The breakage brake

Redemption is what members want. The programme is funded on points nobody uses. So member success and programme margin are wired in opposition.

Realized value+ Redemption rate− Breakage revenue+ Margin headroom+ Offer generosity↩ Realized value
JoannOlaJosh · unresolved

The only brake whose minus is arithmetic rather than behavioural: a point that gets used cannot also go unused. You cannot design it away — you can only stop scoring on it.

B · CONSENT3 of 6

The permission brake

Personalization runs on permission it can spend. Push intensity too hard, members feel watched, consent narrows, and the signal the engine runs on thins out.

Personalization intensity+ Perceived intrusion− Consent granted+ Permissioned signal+ Decisioning relevance↩ Intensity
JoannLiaJosh · as leverage

The only brake that acts on the engine's fuel rather than its output. It caps how hard R1 can be pushed before it starts eating itself.

B · DELIVERY3 of 6 · partial

The value-gap brake

Value that was promised but never visibly arrived. Uncertainty builds, friction rises, and effort goes into detecting and resolving what should have worked the first time.

Unresolved value+ Uncertainty & friction+ Detection effort+ Status clarity+ Realized value− Unresolved value
HenrySlyJoann · as a state machine

Henry and Sly describe the same breakdown from opposite ends — Henry from the member's uncertainty, Sly from the operation's capacity. Both land on unresolved value cutting realization and trust. Marked partial because the cause differs even though the effect matches.

R · ADOPTIONJosh

Connected-data adoption

Not a brake — a second engine, and the one loop Josh’s write-up genuinely supports even though he never drew it. Showing a member what connecting an account returns gives them a reason to connect.

Value of connecting, shown+ Consent granted+ Combined signals+ Decisioning relevance+ Realized value↩ Value shown
Josh

The hook is a concrete, immediate benefit — cash value shown before the member is asked to connect anything. The barrier is low trust in consumer-driven banking. Zero negative links, so it compounds.

A fifth loop — partner coverage (engagement → partner-attributable spend → anchor partner retention → earn-and-redeem breadth → everyday relevance) — appears in only one map. It is also the only loop anyone claims is currently spinning backwards: Sobeys 2022, Shell 2026. Least redundant, least examined.

Settled · 10 September

One set of loop codes

Everyone’s personal numbering is retired. Three of us had used R2 for three different mechanisms, and B2 meant two different loops in two maps — so no shared register could have been built on them.

The nine canonical codes above (TR1–TR6, TB1–TB3) are now the only identifiers. Your original code is kept against your name in the data so you can still find your own work.

Material

Decide what organizes the system

Two candidate objectives are close enough to be mistaken for each other. Realized value — did the promised value arrive and become usable — is measurable from system events alone. Member goal achievement requires the member to state a goal, and requires consent to measure against it.

Nesting is the least destructive option: realized value as the measurable proxy, goal achievement as the intent it serves. It needs testing against members who never set a goal.

Resolvable · sequencing, not a choice

Where the product starts

With Josh’s starting capability confirmed, the positions are genuinely different — and they turn out to sit in dependency order. You cannot detect that value is missing until you know what should have posted, and you cannot personalize credibly until delivery has earned the consent personalization runs on.

Consolidated from six independently produced Milestone 2 submissions — Joann, Sly, Henry, Josh, Lia and Ola. Loop alignment and variable aliasing are analytical judgements, recorded in full so they can be contested.

Convergence is not evidence. Five people independently drawing the same loop is a strong signal that the structure holds. It is not empirical support for the claims inside it, and nothing here has been promoted to "supported" on the strength of agreement alone.

Note on scope. These nine loops are a deliberate simplification of the full systems map, not a competing version of it. The map holds twenty-one components, five regions and the boundary; the loops hold only the closed causal paths through Realized Value. Where the map distinguishes Member-Only, Member-Client and Client-Only, the loops collapse all three into participation. Where the map separates products, data, channels and service, the loops carry only the signal those components produce. The map answers what the system is made of; the loops answer why it behaves as it does. Both are needed and neither replaces the other.
